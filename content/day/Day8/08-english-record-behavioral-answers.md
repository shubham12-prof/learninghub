# 08 - English: Record Five Behavioral Answers (60 to 90 Seconds Each)

> **Time:** ~90 min | **Type:** Speaking practice | **Target:** **5 recorded answers**, each 60 to 90 seconds, reviewed with the checklist below
> I can't hear audio. You record and review; if you paste a transcript here, I can give written feedback on grammar, clarity and structure. The model answers in `07` run **116 to 192 words, which is about 50 to 82 seconds at a calm 140 words per minute** (counted by script).

## 1. The one idea

Behavioral answers are told **aloud**, in the **past tense**, in a **conversational but organised** way. Spoken English is different from written English: shorter sentences, a clear order, and pauses. The goal is not perfect grammar; it is to be **easy to follow**.

## 2. Plan: which 5 answers to record

| #   | Answer                                      | Target length | Words      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ---------- |
| 1   | Tell me about yourself                      | 60 s          | 140 to 160 |
| 2   | A challenging technical problem (STAR)      | 80 s          | 170 to 200 |
| 3   | A disagreement or a mistake (STAR)          | 80 s          | 170 to 200 |
| 4   | Why this company / role?                    | 45 s          | 100 to 120 |
| 5   | Your weakness **or** why should we hire you | 45 s          | 100 to 120 |

At 140 words per minute: 60 s is about 140 words, 90 s is about 210 words.

## 3. Step by step

1. **Write bullet points, not sentences.** For each answer note the STAR keywords, one number, and the lesson. For example: `S: big body -> 500 | T: should be 4xx | A: log, handler, test | R: 413, 19 tests | L: test bad input`.
2. **Say it once without recording** and fix the parts where you got stuck.
3. **Record** all 5 in one go, with a 3-second pause before each answer. Don't restart for small mistakes.
4. **Listen at normal speed** with the checklist. Mark: the **longest sentence**, every **filler**, every **"we"** that should be **"I"**.
5. **Fix three things**, then re-record only the answers below 3 on the rubric.
6. **Make a transcript** with phone dictation or a speech-to-text tool; count words and divide by the seconds to get your pace. Paste it to me for feedback if you like.
7. Repeat tomorrow (**spaced practice**) and compare the two recordings.

## 4. Review checklist (score each answer 1 to 5)

| Check            | What good sounds like                                           |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Structure**    | I can hear S, T, A, R and a lesson                              |
| **"I" not "we"** | the listener knows what **I** did                               |
| **Specifics**    | a number, a tool or a concrete detail                           |
| **Length**       | within the target (not under 45 s, not over 100 s)              |
| **Pace**         | 120 to 160 words per minute, with pauses between parts          |
| **Fillers**      | fewer than 3 per minute ("um", "like", "you know", "basically") |
| **Tone**         | calm and positive; no blaming; the ending sounds finished       |
| **Grammar**      | past tense for the story, present for facts                     |

## 5. Grammar for storytelling (the usual traps)

| Situation             | Don't say                                 | Say                                                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Finished past event   | "Last month I have fixed a bug."          | "Last month I **fixed** a bug."                      |
| Experience up to now  | "I worked here since 2024."               | "I **have worked** here since 2024."                 |
| Past habit            | "I was using to test it manually."        | "I **used to** test it manually."                    |
| Sequence              | "After I found the bug, I was fixing it." | "After I **found** the bug, I **fixed** it."         |
| Result with a number  | "It's reduced from 2 seconds to 150 ms."  | "It **dropped** from 2 seconds to 150 milliseconds." |
| Opinions              | "I am agree with him."                    | "I **agreed** with him." / "I **agree**."            |
| Countable/uncountable | "I got many feedbacks."                   | "I got a lot of **feedback**."                       |
| Preposition           | "I discussed about the design."           | "I discussed **the design**."                        |
| Third person          | "The server return an error."             | "The server **returns** an error."                   |
| Article               | "I fixed bug in login."                   | "I fixed **a** bug in **the** login."                |

## 6. Useful phrases

**Starting a story:** "A good example is..." · "One time when..." · "In my last project..."

**Describing your part:** "My responsibility was..." · "I took ownership of..." · "I decided to..." · "I started by..." · "Then I..." · "Finally I..."

**Showing results:** "As a result..." · "This reduced... from X to Y." · "The feedback was..." · "The tests went from 18 to 19."

**The lesson:** "The main thing I learned was..." · "Since then, I always..." · "If it happened again, I would..."

**Disagreeing politely:** "I see your point, and I'd like to add..." · "What if we tried a small experiment?"

**Buying a moment:** "That's a good question; let me think for a second." · "Let me give you a specific example."

## 7. Fixing common speaking problems

| Problem                                   | Fix                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Speaking too fast                         | pause for one full second at the end of every sentence; breathe                   |
| Too many "um"                             | **pause silently** instead; a pause sounds thoughtful                             |
| Going blank                               | go back to the STAR letter you remember and continue from there                   |
| Too long                                  | cut the Situation to one sentence and remove the second example                   |
| Monotone voice                            | stress key words (numbers, "I decided", the result) and smile slightly            |
| Unclear pronunciation of a technical term | say it slowly once, then use it normally (see `05` in Day 7)                      |
| Nervousness                               | do the 3-second pause, drink water, record again; the third take is always better |

## 8. Predict: fix these sentences

**Q1** "In my previous project we are having a problem with the speed, so we was trying to cache it."
**Q2** "I am agree that I made a mistake, and I learn from it."
**Q3** "The result was good, I think."

## 9. Answers

- **Q1:** "In my previous project we **had** a performance problem, so I **tried** caching." (Past tense; singular "problem"; say what **I** did.)
- **Q2:** "I **agree** that I made a mistake, and I **learned** from it." Even better: "I made a mistake, and it taught me to test the empty state first."
- **Q3:** "As a result, the page loaded in **150 ms instead of 2 seconds**, and the reviewer approved it the next day." (Evidence, not a guess.)

## Recap

- Behavioral answers are **spoken stories**: 60 to 90 seconds, STAR, **past tense**, **"I"**.
- Record, **listen**, fix **three** things, record again.
- Use **pauses instead of fillers**; give **one number** per story.
- Practise on two different days: spacing makes it stick.
